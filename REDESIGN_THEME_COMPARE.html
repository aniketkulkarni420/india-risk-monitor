Verdict

Light wins the default slot. Look at the headline in both — on light, the eye lands immediately on the sentence (the highest-value piece of content). On dark, the eye competes between the headline and the bright "50" pin on the pulse line.

The pulse line is more readable on light — the green→amber→red gradient lands as semantic information rather than as a stylistic flourish. Markers don't fight the bright pulse for attention.

But dark is genuinely good too — it signals "pro tool" and several power-user moments (late-night, comparing against Bloomberg in another tab) actively prefer it. Hence: ship both, default light, persist user choice in localStorage.

Color tokens · what changes between themes

TokenLight valueDark valueWhere used
--bg#ffffff#0c1118Page background
--text#0f1419#e4e7ebPrimary text
--mute#6b7280#8b95a3Secondary text
--line#e6e8ed#1f2733Borders / dividers
--card#ffffff#131820Card surface (light = lighter than bg; dark = lighter than bg too — the "lighter card on darker bg" rule from the audit report)
--accent-warn#c2410c#f59e0bAmber/warning. Light theme uses a darker amber for contrast on white (pure #f59e0b is unreadable on white).
--accent-danger#dc2626#ef4444Red/shock
--accent-good#16a34a#4ade80Green/safe
--accent-info#1a73e8#60a5faBlue/info/links

The tricky one is amber: dashboards always reach for #f59e0b, but it has 2.4:1 contrast on white — WCAG fails. Light theme needs the darker burnt-orange #c2410c (4.5:1, AA-pass). Most "dark-converted-to-light" dashboards trip on this.
